Living in Germany: Essential Guide & Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers on German taxation, 2026 Kindergeld benefit rates, Berlin holidays, and rental procedures.

💡 How is German net salary (Netto) calculated from gross salary (Brutto)?

In Germany, your monthly net income is calculated by deducting statutory social insurances (Health Insurance ~14.6% + additional ~1.7%, Pension 18.6%, Unemployment 2.6%, Long-Term Care 4.0% with childless surcharge/discounts) and Wage Tax (Lohnsteuer under EStG § 32a progressive brackets) according to your Tax Class (Steuerklasse I to VI). In Berlin, church tax (Kirchensteuer) is 9% of income tax if registered with a recognized church.

👨‍👩‍👧 What is the official Kindergeld (Child Benefit) rate in 2026?

As of 2026, statutory Kindergeld is €259 per month per child (legally enacted), up from €250 in 2023–2024 and €255 in 2025. For upcoming years, the German federal government has announced / proposed increases to €267 per child/month for 2027 and €272 per child/month for 2028 (subject to final parliamentary enactment; Source: BMF / Familienkasse). The Familienkasse pays this benefit directly to parents regardless of income for all eligible children up to age 18 (or up to age 25 if studying or in vocational training).

📅 What are the statutory public holidays in Berlin for 2026?

Berlin observes 10 statutory public holidays (gesetzliche Feiertage): New Year's Day (Jan 1), International Women's Day / Frauentag (March 8), Good Friday, Easter Monday, Ascension Day, Whit Monday, German Unity Day (Oct 3), and Christmas (Dec 25–26). Unlike southern states like Bavaria or Baden-Württemberg, Berlin does not celebrate Epiphany or Corpus Christi, but uniquely recognizes Frauentag.

🏠 What is the difference between Kaltmiete (Cold Rent) and Warmmiete (Warm Rent)?

Kaltmiete (Grundmiete) is the pure base rent for the empty property space. Warmmiete (Gesamtmiete) equals Kaltmiete plus Nebenkosten (Betriebskosten), which covers heating, water, trash disposal, building cleaning, and elevator maintenance. Note that private household electricity, internet, and the statutory broadcasting contribution (Rundfunkbeitrag, formerly commonly called GEZ, at €18.36/month per dwelling) are billed separately. Under broadcasting contribution regulations (§ 2 RBStV), the contribution is levied strictly per dwelling (Wohnung)—meaning roommates in a flatshare (WG) or couples only pay once for the shared household.
